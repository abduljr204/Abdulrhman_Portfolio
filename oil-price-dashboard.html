CASE STUDY · POWER BI · ENERGY PRICES

Global Oil Price Analysis Dashboard.

A Power BI dashboard that compares four crude oil benchmarks in July 2025: WTI, Brent, the OPEC Basket and Libya's Es Sider grade. It uses KPI cards, daily trend lines, a daily price table and a comparison of the first and second halves of the month.

Role
Sole Analyst and Designer
Tools
Power BI ,Excel , SQL
Data covered
Daily prices, 1–30 July 2025
Built
2025/08/01

01

Business question

This dashboard answers: “Which crude oils increased or decreased in price this month compared with the previous month, and what market developments may explain those changes?” It was developed for the Minister of Oil and Gas and other ministry stakeholders to support monthly crude oil price reporting, executive briefings and informed discussions about market trends.

02

Data sources

The dashboard uses daily prices in US dollars per barrel for four benchmarks. The KPI cards, line chart and daily table cover 15–30 July 2025. The Es Sider area chart covers all of July.

Benchmarks and sources
Benchmark What it is
WTI US light crude benchmark
Brent North Sea / international benchmark
OPEC Basket Average of OPEC members' crudes
Es Sider Libyan export crude grade

03

Cleaning & preparation

I collected crude oil prices from OilPrice, OPEC and Libya’s National Oil Corporation (NOC), then entered them manually into the dataset. I handled missing price entries and corrected formatting issues in the Arabic-language data to make it consistent for analysis and reporting.

04

Modelling & calculations

The KPI cards show each benchmark's average daily price for 15–30 July. All four card values match the averages of the daily table to one decimal place. The comparison table sets the average for 1–15 July against the average for 15–30 July.

05

Dashboard design

  • KPI cards: average price for each benchmark, colour-coded to match the chart lines.
  • Line chart: daily prices for all four benchmarks on one axis, for direct comparison.
  • Daily price table: the exact values behind the chart.
  • Period comparison: first versus second half of July, with the difference.
  • Es Sider area chart: the Libyan grade across the whole month.

06

What the data shows

  • All four benchmarks ended the period higher than they started, rising 17% (WTI) to 21% (Es Sider) between 15 and 30 July.
  • Brent had the highest average ($77.8) and WTI the lowest ($73.5).
  • Es Sider was priced below Brent on every date shown, by $3.3 per barrel on average.
  • Prices rose most sharply on 22 July and again between 24 and 28 July.

07

Limitations

  • Short window. The main views cover only 12 dates in the second half of July 2025, so they show short-term movement, not long-term trends.
  • Descriptive only. The dashboard shows what prices did, not why. It doesn't include supply, demand or news data.
